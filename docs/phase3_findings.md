# Phase 3 — Agency Performance Findings

## Executive Summary

The validated synthetic snapshot covers 44 complete reporting months from January
2023 through August 2026. In August 2026, 338 of 368 eligible agents produced at
least one policy (91.85%). They wrote 1,662 new policies with UGX 5.050 billion in
annualized premium. Compared with July, policy volume fell 4.15% and annualized
premium fell 5.13%; policies per producing agent also moved from 5.07 to 4.92.

August achieved 34.45% of the aggregate policy target and 72.41% of the aggregate
premium target. Results varied substantially by region, unit and agent. This is a
descriptive analysis of synthetic data: it does not establish causes, measure
persistency or attribute policies directly to leads.

not maturity-equivalent.

## 2. Monthly Performance Trend

The complete-month trend runs from January 2023 through August 2026 and retains
zero-production calendar months. August recorded 1,662 policies, down 4.15% from
July's 1,734, while annualized premium declined 5.13% from UGX 5.323 billion to
UGX 5.050 billion. The producing-agent ratio remained above 91% in both months,
but output per producer fell from 5.07 to 4.92 policies. September 2026 is
incomplete and is not included.

## 4. Agency Unit Performance

All 12 units had complete policy and premium target coverage in August. Kampala
North recorded the most policies (236), the highest policies per producer (6.94)
and the highest target achievement: 48.66% for policies and 105.32% for premium.
Mbarara had a 100% producing-agent ratio and 6.44 policies per producer. Entebbe
also had a 100% producing-agent ratio, with 4.23 policies per producer.

Gulu recorded the lowest policy and premium target achievement (20.11% and
42.67%) and 2.85 policies per producer. Masaka had the lowest producing-agent
ratio (83.33%). The unit results show that participation and productivity are
separate dimensions: a high producing-agent ratio does not by itself guarantee
high target achievement.

## 5. Agent Productivity and Target Achievement

The highest August policy counts were 32 each for A0098 (Lira) and A0260
(Kampala North), followed by 31 for A0166 (Kampala North). The ten agents displayed
in the executed notebook produced between 20 and 32 policies each. All ten met or
exceeded their premium targets; eight were policy high achievers and two were
policy target achievers.

At Agency level, 32 of 368 eligible agents achieved at least 100% of policy target,
while 90 achieved at least 100% of premium target. These are separate measures:
policy volume achievement does not imply premium achievement, or vice versa.

## 8. Agent Activation

First-30-day activation means the first observed policy was issued in the half-open
interval from join date through day 29. The denominator includes only recruits
whose history is observable from January 2023, whose 30-day window is mature by
the snapshot, and who remained in service through day 29.

Of 400 recruits, 126 were left-censored because they joined before January 2023.
There were no immature 30-day windows and no early exits in this snapshot. The
true analysis population was therefore 274 eligible recruits; 257 activated in
their first 30 days, a 93.80% activation ratio. Median observed time to first
policy was seven days among observed producers. First observed policy must not be
interpreted as first-ever policy for pre-2023 recruits.

## 10. Production Concentration

For August, the top group contained 34 agents: `CEIL(10% × 338 producing agents)`.
Ranked independently for each measure, the top group produced 36.58% of policies
and 37.08% of annualized premium. In July, 35 selected agents produced 36.27% of
policies and 36.19% of annualized premium. Concentration describes how production
is distributed; it is not automatically negative and does not establish retention
or departure risk.

## 11. Management Priorities

1. **Observation:** August policy and premium production declined from July while
   more than 91% of eligible agents still produced. **Diagnosis:** Participation
   alone does not account for the shortfall; output per producer was also lower.
   **Business implication:** An Agency-wide activation response would address only
   one dimension of performance. **Possible management action:** Review producer
   output and target assumptions while focusing activation follow-up on the 30
   non-producing agents.

2. **Observation:** Unit results differ across participation, productivity and
   policy-versus-premium achievement. **Diagnosis:** A single volume ranking blends
   team size with per-producer output and product mix. **Business implication:** A
   uniform intervention could miss the dimension in which each unit differs.
   **Possible management action:** Use the unit participation/productivity view and
   both target measures to prioritize manager reviews, then inspect agent detail.

3. **Observation:** Gulu had the lowest target achievement and productivity, while
   Masaka had the lowest producing-agent ratio. **Diagnosis:** The recorded metrics
   point to different immediate gaps but do not reveal their causes. **Business
   implication:** Treating both units as the same issue could misdirect support.
   **Possible management action:** Review Gulu's producer output and Masaka's
   non-producer cases with their managers before selecting any intervention.

4. **Observation:** The top 10% groups supplied about 37% of August production.
   **Diagnosis:** Production is meaningfully concentrated, although the analysis
   contains no evidence that this is harmful. **Business implication:** Management
   should understand both high-producer practices and the breadth of the producer
   base. **Possible management action:** Monitor concentration alongside producer
   count and investigate transferable practices without penalizing top producers.

5. **Observation:** Newer tenure and lower recorded-grade groups show higher
   descriptive productivity in the synthetic snapshot. **Diagnosis:** Service
   windows, current attributes and generator construction can shape this pattern.
   **Business implication:** The result is not evidence for changing recruitment,
   grade or incentive policy. **Possible management action:** Validate the pattern
   with longitudinal operational data before testing a management response.

## 12. Analytical Limitations

- All data is synthetic; findings demonstrate the analytical workflow rather than
  actual insurer performance.
- Policy history begins in January 2023, creating left censoring for earlier recruits.
- Agent transfer history is unavailable.
- Current unit, region and grade are used retrospectively rather than reconstructed
  as of each historical month.
- Lead-stage timestamps are unavailable.
- Lead-stage statuses are not guaranteed to be nested.
- Policies have no `lead_id`, so direct lead-to-policy attribution is unavailable.
- Annualized premium is normalized contract value, not collected cash or revenue.
- Current policy status is not a persistency measure; Phase 3 does not calculate
  persistency.
- Phase 3 contains descriptive, not causal, analysis.
- September 2026 is incomplete and excluded from complete-month comparisons.
- Recent lead cohorts have less time to convert than older cohorts.
- First-30-day activation excludes left-censored, immature-window and early-exit
  recruits from its denominator; exclusion categories can overlap in other snapshots.
